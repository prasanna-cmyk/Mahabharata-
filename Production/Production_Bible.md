# Mahabharata — 4K series production bible

**Working language:** Kannada spoken narration and dialogue, as explicitly requested by the user; English visual prompts and production instructions. A language change requires a translation and pronunciation pass before recording.

**Source scope:** the supplied archive contains four OCR text files: this edition's complete numbered Adiparva1–237 and Sabhaparva1–24. The fourth volume ends after Jarasandha's defeat, the imprisoned kings' release and the return to Indraprastha. An asset appearing in this bible does not establish that its later events appear in the supplied text. Retain source previews as previews; additional parvas need additional source books.

**Document status:** practical adaptation specification and reusable visual development assets. The source is OCR; the underlying printed pages have not been visually checked. Spelling, Sanskrit verses, section boundaries and damaged sentences need verification before the final voice session. Script prose is an original adaptation, not a claim to reproduce every printed sentence.

## 1. What the audience must always understand

Each scene must make four things clear: who is present, where they are, what each person wants, and what changes. Use one clear narrative action per shot. Establish a location before cutting into conversation; establish a relationship before relying on a family title. Introduce people by name and relationship once, then use their name consistently. Recap only the facts the next scene needs.

Distinguish the source's outer storytelling frame from the inner one. **Ugrashravas Sauti tells Shaunaka and the gathered sages at Naimisha forest. Vaishampayana tells Janamejaya at the king's sacrificial assembly.** Within that account, historical and mythic events unfold. Do not stage Ugrashravas as the speaker in Janamejaya's court, or transfer Vaishampayana's dialogue to Shaunaka. When a character within a story narrates another account, add a third frame marker in the script and return to the exact speaker who opened it.

The opening frame should identify the current speaker on screen and in narration. A frame transition gets a distinct sound bridge and a location card; subsequent transitions use the same visual grammar. The frame scenes have their own ongoing time and reactions. A cut back to the listening assembly should answer a dramatic need, not serve as arbitrary filler.

### Editorial layers

| Layer | Treatment |
|---|---|
| Publisher material, edition introduction, acknowledgments, contents | Preserve their existence in the source ledger. Summarize their relevant function in a short edition prologue or companion notes; do not put a publisher's words into an ancient character's mouth. |
| Epic narrative and embedded stories | Dramatize with original Kannada narration and dialogue, tracing every event to a source block. Preserve causes, consequences, kinship and stated motives. |
| The edition's Madhva commentary or doctrinal interpretation | Explicitly introduce it as this edition's interpretation or a named commentary perspective. Keep it in an identified commentary segment unless the source itself makes it part of the story. |
| Genealogies, catalogues, teachings and summaries | Use intelligible narrated cards, portraits, maps and short explanatory scenes. Log each retained name or proposition and any grouping. Do not call a grouped summary a word-for-word rendering. |
| OCR corruption or uncertain reading | Tag it for review. Do not invent a missing event, name or quotation to make the sentence sound complete. |

Devotional reverence can coexist with dramatic clarity. Avoid treating one edition's doctrinal claims as a universally agreed interpretation. Do not project medieval Madhva-associated dress, sectarian markings or institutions onto every ancient character. All costume and architecture here are coherent epic visual-development choices, not a reconstruction certified as historically exact.

## 2. Series structure and script contract

The narrative production order follows the validated source sequence. A chronology aid can explain an earlier event, but the script must distinguish a flashback from the present. Build each episode around a consequence rather than an arbitrary word count. Do not cut off a vow before its consequences, a curse before its conditions, or a rescue before the audience understands who survived.

Duration is provisional until a Kannada table read. For planning, use roughly 105–135 spoken Kannada words per minute for clear dramatic narration, then measure the actual performer; emotional pauses, Sanskrit recitation, songs and silence change the result. Do not mark a script as a final 22-minute episode on the basis of text length alone. Record actual read duration and add measured action time without counting overlapping speech twice.

Every scene handoff needs:

1. Episode and scene ID; source file and source-block references; adaptation or OCR flags.
2. Story frame, internal chronology, location, time of day and weather.
3. Characters, age stage, relationships the audience already knows and new information introduced.
4. Dramatic objective, obstacle, visible action and resulting change.
5. Kannada narration and dialogue with speaker labels; a literal English production gloss where needed.
6. Shot list with image asset IDs, action-specific prompts, screen direction, estimated duration and sound cues.
7. Continuity entering and leaving the scene: wounds, costume, held objects, promises, knowledge, location and elapsed time.

A narrator may explain a thought that the source attributes to a character. Newly written dialogue must not silently alter a decision, prophecy, theological claim or relationship. Mark adaptation dialogue in the script metadata. A dramatic silent beat can illustrate an emotion without pretending to be a verbatim source statement.

## 3. Delivery format and the meaning of 4K

Default delivery is **3840 × 2160, 16:9, progressive, 24 fps, SDR Rec.709**. This is a delivery specification, not a claim about the native resolution of every generated asset. Use a different frame rate only after the whole project agrees; mixing 24, 25 and 30 fps creates unnecessary cadence problems. A 2.39:1 crop is optional for a specifically approved alternate master, not the default because it discards useful 16:9 composition.

Record each asset's actual pixel dimensions and origin. If an image or video is generated below 4K, label it **upscaled to UHD** in the internal manifest. Never call upscaled footage native 4K. Render and inspect the final UHD master at full resolution after compositing, grain and titles. Fine jewelry, fingers, eyes, lips, cloth edges, smoke and foliage are common failure areas. An attractive thumbnail is not a full-resolution approval.

Keep character faces and story-critical actions away from the lower subtitle region. Use the central 90% of the frame for indispensable titles and credits; check the actual delivery platform's requirements before distribution. Compose the principal subject with enough headroom and side clearance for gentle reframing. Save text-free masters so Kannada titles and subtitles can be typeset accurately in post.

Maintain a high-quality edit master and a separate distribution encode. Audio handoff is 48 kHz, 24-bit WAV with separate dialogue, narration, music and effects stems. The final codec, loudness and caption packaging must match the chosen distributor, not an assumed universal standard.

## 4. Visual world

Nature is part of the drama. A low river exposes a crossing; dense reeds conceal a listener; approaching rain motivates shelter; leaf shadows divide a private conversation; a dusty courtyard reveals approaching feet. Do not add waterfalls, glowing particles, peacocks and lotus ponds to every shot. Natural details should establish location, season or emotional contrast without obscuring the action.

The overall image has believable human anatomy, textured woven cotton and linen-like fabrics, restrained silk accents, carved wood, fired earth, stone, copper and bronze. Gold appears as a controlled status detail. Avoid an indiscriminate wall of gold, glassy skin, contemporary cosmetics, fantasy plate armour and furniture that changes culture from scene to scene.

| Region or story setting | Visual continuity |
|---|---|
| Naimisha listening forest | Broadleaf canopy, leaf litter, fig roots, filtered green daylight, ochre mats, shallow water nearby; calm horizontal camera language. |
| Janamejaya's sacrificial assembly | Dry prepared ceremonial ground, timber shelter, clear paths, vessels and seated witnesses; controlled smoke and warm fire light, with the speaking area visible. |
| Ganga river settlements | Silt banks, reeds, weathered moorings, practical boats, humid blue-grey dawn and copper evening; water level remains consistent across a scene. |
| Hastinapura | River-plain city, sandstone and timber, shaded courts, restrained geometric carving, working streets, managed gardens; authority expressed by scale and order. |
| Himalayan foothills and retreats | Layered ridges, stream-fed clearings, cool air, stone paths and vegetation suited to altitude. Snow remains distant unless the scene is explicitly high enough. |
| Panchala | Broad agricultural plain, ochre earth, spacious timber-and-stone courts and orderly training grounds; event decoration concentrated at the actual venue. |
| Ekachakra and travelling settlements | Worn domestic materials, grain baskets, patched textiles and lived-in courtyards; poverty does not mean dirt caricatures or featureless misery. |
| Khandava | A distinct living forest with canopy, undergrowth, nesting and watercourses. If a burning sequence is in the verified source, preserve the forest's established layout and the event's gravity. |
| Indraprastha and Maya's hall | Begin with the site's documented narrative stage. The completed hall uses repeated architectural anchors; apparent water and floor illusions obey planned spatial rules. |

Choose a season per sequence, not per generated image. Keep rain wetness, river level, vegetation and shadows consistent through consecutive shots. Travel should change landscape progressively. Use a map only when it clarifies relative direction; label exact ancient borders and geographic placements as interpretive where uncertain.

## 5. Character identity and costume continuity

The JSON asset bible supplies design anchors. These are original casting designs, not textual claims about exact faces, body sizes or clothing. Skin tone must not encode caste, moral worth, violence or spiritual rank. Use a natural range of South Asian complexions across all social and moral roles. Do not make antagonists uniformly darker, scarred or deformed. A moral turn does not change a person's face.

For every recurring role, approve a front view, both profiles, three-quarter view, full figure, six restrained expressions, hair diagram, costume swatches and primary props. A text prompt alone will not preserve an identity. Reuse approved visual references and retain the actual tool settings when the generation system supports them. Seed reuse is an aid, not an identity guarantee.

Separate each life stage into an asset variant: child, adolescent, young adult, mature and elderly only where the story requires it. The jaw, eye spacing and other identity anchors should remain recognizable. Never use an adult romantic body design for a child. Cast young-adult marriage scenes as adults while preserving source-age questions in the adaptation notes rather than quietly making a historical claim.

Costume follows circumstance: fisher household, royal court, training ground, forest residence, disguise or exile. A crown does not remain on a sleeping traveller. Bowstrings, quivers, carried vessels, jewelry and sandals have a defined state at every cut. Do not change a character's ceremonial marking or sacred thread randomly. Use such elements only where the source and the approved cultural review support them.

Dhritarashtra's blindness is not a cue for a vacant face or magical white eyes. Gandhari's blindfold must stay tied in the same manner when it is present. Karna's distinctive protective attributes require a timeline check before every scene; do not add later losses early. Bhishma's hair and facial age should match the story period. Krishna's presentation should follow the narrative function of the moment; overt divine iconography is a deliberate scene choice, not the default for every social encounter.

## 6. Asset and shot prompting workflow

`asset_bible.json` contains reusable key-art prompts. They describe identity or place; they are **not a substitute for each scene's action prompt**. The episode script must bind the right assets to a specific dramatic action. Generate the approved character reference first, then the location reference, then the shot. Do not attempt a crowded multi-character action before both the identities and blocking are fixed.

Use this order in each image prompt:

**Reference IDs → who and age stage → observable action → geography and weather → costume and held objects → blocking → lens/framing → motivated light → texture → continuity exclusions.**

For example: “Use approved CH_ARJUNA youth and LO_TRAINING references. Arjuna stands at the marked near end of the same earth practice lane, left shoulder toward camera, bow raised toward the fixed target at screen right; the bow is held in his left hand and the drawn string in his right. Other pupils remain soft in the background. Late-afternoon light enters from camera left through the existing trees; dust rises only around his planted right foot. Medium side view, restrained depth of field, no extra hands, no second bow, no changing target.” This is a production example; use it only in a scene that the source actually supports.

Prompt quality comes from specific visual decisions. Prefer one readable subject and one action. Avoid contradictory combinations such as aerial close-up, extreme shallow focus with every distant leaf sharp, or moonlit noon. Camera focal lengths are creative approximations unless the generation tool models a real lens. Use one focal range per shot family: wide for geography, normal for relationships, longer for intimate decisions.

**Image-to-video:** start with an approved still. Specify one subject movement, one camera movement or a locked camera, and a few secondary environmental movements. Typical starting clips are 4–8 seconds, adjusted to the tool and edit. Complex fighting, crowds, transformations and hand exchanges need shorter controlled setups, clean inserts and deliberate cuts. A long prompt listing many sequential events is not a reliable long take.

Separate movement direction from screen direction. If the travellers enter from screen left, keep their progress toward screen right until an establishing shot motivates a reversal. Maintain eyelines and the conversation axis. Never repair continuity by reversing text-bearing props, sacred markings or a character's established weapon hand without review.

Before generating more clips, assemble a still-image animatic with temporary Kannada speech. Resolve confusing geography, missing cause-and-effect and episode length there. Lock the script for a sequence, then create its final shots. New image variants should fix a named problem, not merely multiply choices.

## 7. Sound, music and performance

Speak Kannada naturally and clearly. Use a pronunciation sheet containing the approved Kannada spelling, Sanskrit form when relevant, and a short phonetic guide for every recurring name. A Kannada editor should review sandhi, honorifics, gender and number agreement, narration register and character-specific diction. Distinguish a source quotation from newly written dramatic speech in the recording script.

Narration carries transitions and inner context; dialogue carries decisions and conflict. Avoid narrating exactly what the actor and image already communicate. Pause after a new family relationship, a difficult name or a major vow. Use measured emotion without a permanently declamatory tone.

Develop sound motifs by narrative function: an airy forest bed for listening frames, restrained ceremony sounds for Janamejaya's assembly, river movement for Ganga settings, and a recurring sparse musical idea for vows. Instrument choices are artistic proposals, not claims that a specific modern instrument existed in the epic's period. Keep action effects intelligible and music subordinate to speech.

Maintain room or environmental tone under edits. Place footsteps on the surface shown. Cloth, jewelry and weapons should sound like their depicted materials. Crowd presence can support scale without drowning out an individual. For ritual scenes, verify pronunciations and do not fabricate sacred recitation merely to fill a soundtrack.

For an initial online stereo review, a practical starting point is around **−16 LUFS integrated with true peak at or below −1 dBTP**; this is a house proposal, not a universal platform rule. Measure intelligibility on headphones, a television and a phone speaker. Adjust to the actual distributor's specification before mastering. Deliver clean stems and avoid clipping from stacked drums or effects.

## 8. Subtitles, accessibility and graphics

Deliver Kannada captions and, if required, a separate English translation track. Use Unicode text and a font licensed for the intended delivery. Do not ask an image model to draw Kannada text. Typeset subtitles, relationship cards, maps and chapter headings in the edit.

Use at most two subtitle lines and split at meaningful phrase boundaries. Test Kannada grapheme clusters rather than treating each Unicode code point as a visible letter. Use actual reading checks for Kannada; an initial English target of roughly 15–17 characters per second can guide translation subtitles but is not an automatic Kannada rule. Keep names consistent across subtitles, narration, thumbnails and credits. Captions identify important non-speech sound when needed.

Genealogy graphics should reveal one relationship at a time. Use portraits and explicit “parent”, “child”, “sibling” or “spouse” labels rather than relying on colour alone. Keep a persistent legend for any adopted and biological relationships relevant to a scene. Do not use a decorative family tree so dense that the viewer cannot follow the spoken explanation.

## 10. Approval and completeness checks

The following are production quality gates, not requests for additional permission:

- **Source:** every source block is mapped to a scripted scene, an explicitly identified companion segment, a deliberate summary or an unresolved item. Missing blocks cannot disappear behind a blanket “complete” label. Introductory matter and commentary have their own dispositions.
- **Narrative:** frame speakers, family relationships, chronology, vows, curses, boons, conditions and consequences remain consistent. A name introduced in a catalogue is not silently assumed to be familiar later.
- **Language:** Kannada read-through completed; uncertain OCR words resolved or clearly withheld from quotation; subtitles agree with recorded audio.
- **Visual:** approved identities and life stages; coherent clothing and held objects; stable screen direction; accurate hand and weapon interactions; no text corruption, unintended logos or modern intrusions.
- **Motion:** no face drift, disappearing limbs, bowstring glitches, sliding feet, teleporting props, reversed gestures or unnaturally repeating water/fire.
- **Sound:** voice intelligible; no clipping or missing ambience; music and effects licensed or otherwise authorized; sacred text pronunciation checked when used.
- **Delivery:** actual 3840 × 2160 master inspected; native versus upscaled origins documented; frames, duration and captions checked after export; checksum and version manifest saved.

“All-inclusive” is earned by the coverage audit, not by the number of episodes or image prompts. This bible covers a reusable production method. Final completeness, exact episode timings, finished images, recorded speech and edited video remain separate deliverables and must be reported at their actual verified status.

This edition is the Palimaru Matha Madhva (Dvaita) edition: Vedavyasa's Mahabharata with
Shrīmad Anandatīrtha's Mahābhārata Tatparyanirnaya and Shrī Vādirāja's Lakshyaalankara,
published by Tattvasamshodhana Samsat, and the four anugraha-sandesha of Pejawara,
Adamaru, Palimaru and Sode mathas. ALL keyframes, character references and location
references must stay consistent with Madhva Sampradaya where a scene involves a matha,
temple, deity, priest, ritual or commentary context:

- Deity: Lord Krishna as Parabrahman — dark/blue-complexioned, four-armed (shankha,
  chakra, gada, padma), peacock-feather crown (the Madhva embleme), peacock mount where
  shown. No generic multi-armed deity stand-ins in Madhva-context scenes.
- Matha architecture: Udupi Ashta-Matha style — laterite stone, sloping tiled roofs,
  dhvaja-stambha with saffron Madhva flags, tulsi courtyard, brass lamps and bells.
  The four named mathas (Pejawara/Adhokshaja, Adamaru, Palimaru, Sode) are distinct
  locations; keep each visually stable across its scenes.
- Priests: saffron dhoti and angavastram, white upper garment, Madhva kumkum tilaka,
  sikha; no other-order tilaka, no other-sect turbans or robes.
- Ritual: Vaishnava kirtana with talam (cymbals), mridangam, veena; sandal and turmeric;
  no Shaiva aarti or lingam in Madhva contexts.
- Never mix sampradayas: no Ramanuja/Sri Vaishnava iconography, no Shaiva imagery in

- Sampradaya-neutral scenes (Naimisha rishis, ancient courts, battles, villages) stay
  neutral; the Madhva identity is carried by the edition's paratext scenes, not forced
  onto ancient-history scenes.
- Palette: saffron and gold dominant in religious interiors; deep blue for Krishna;
  ivory/teak/brass for material detail.
- Standing negative-prompt addition for all religious-context scenes:
  "no Shaiva iconography, no lingam, no other sampradaya tilaka or robes, no modern
  tourist temple, no readable text, no watermark."

generation; the Image_Manifest.json records the prompt_version and madhva_context for
